ROC Search Report Fees and Format

An ROC search report is a professional review of specified company information available through MCA records. It is commonly used for initial due diligence, transaction review and corporate record checks.

What can the report cover?

Depending on the agreed scope, it may review company status, directors, charges and selected filed documents. The availability and completeness of records remain subject to the MCA portal.

What affects the fee?

Professional fees depend on the company, search scope, documents, charge review and required analysis. MCA or third-party charges, where applicable, are confirmed separately.

ROC charge search: define the records and questions

If you searched for an ROC charge search, specify that you want charge-related records included in the assignment. Agree whether you need copies of available records or a written analysis with source references. A general company-information search and a detailed charge review need not cover the same work.

Charge-review schedule for your report

Use the following as a working checklist, not an official MCA form. Complete each entry only where supported by the records actually reviewed:

  • Company name and CIN, search date and agreed record cut-off.
  • Charge identifier and the documents reviewed, with retrieval dates.
  • Charge holder, amount and description of secured assets as stated in the source records.
  • Recorded status and any creation, modification or satisfaction records included in the review.
  • Differences between sources, missing documents and questions for the company or intended recipient.

What the search cannot establish on its own

A recorded amount is not a confirmation of today's loan balance. A public-record review does not establish that every liability has been identified or that an asset is free from all claims. Where the decision depends on repayment, release or current security terms, identify the additional evidence required and agree who will obtain it. State inaccessible records as limitations, rather than treating their absence as proof of a clean position.

Available company records are accessed through the official MCA portal. Access conditions and record availability are checked at the time of the search. This checklist describes the proposed review scope, not a legal opinion on a particular charge.

Illustrative ROC search report format

This original outline is a preparation aid, not an MCA-prescribed form, a completed report or a certificate. Adapt it to the agreed purpose and records actually reviewed. Do not use it to make unsupported conclusions about a company.

  1. Assignment: client, purpose, company name or CIN, search date and agreed scope.
  2. Sources reviewed: identify each record, filing period, retrieval date and any unavailable document.
  3. Company particulars: record the details supported by the reviewed sources and flag discrepancies.
  4. Directors and capital: summarise relevant records within scope, with document references.
  5. Charges: describe the charge records reviewed and their recorded status as at the search date.
  6. Filing observations: list documents reviewed, apparent gaps and questions needing clarification.
  7. Limitations and follow-up: explain inaccessible or incomplete records, excluded work and further evidence required.
  8. Report identification: preparer, report date and attachments, with any signing or certification requirements agreed separately.

Example of an evidence-based observation

Illustrative wording: “The document identified in the records schedule was reviewed on the stated search date. The related supporting record was not available within the agreed search scope. Obtain that record before relying on a conclusion about the transaction.” Replace this example with verified facts and precise references; never imply that a missing online record proves non-compliance.

How we scope ROC search report fees

Specify the company or companies, purpose, period, documents required, charge review and requested delivery date. A targeted record search and a broader due-diligence review are different assignments. The written quote should separate professional work from MCA document-access fees and other third-party charges, and explain whether follow-up searches or certified copies are included.

What to send before requesting a report

  • Company name and CIN, if known.
  • Purpose: for example, an internal review or a proposed transaction.
  • The periods, filings or charge records you want examined.
  • Any existing report or specific discrepancy to investigate.
  • The intended recipient and required date.

Confirm any recipient-specific format or certification requirement before work starts. Do not send confidential transaction documents through an initial public enquiry; agree a suitable document-sharing route with the team.
