Records to prepare for an income-tax notice review

Start with the complete communication, the assessment year, response date and current portal status. The review team will confirm the records relevant to the issues raised.

  • Full notice or intimation, including annexures and response instructions.
  • Filed return, acknowledgement and relevant computations.
  • Available tax-information statements, tax-credit records and supporting transaction evidence.
  • Earlier replies, acknowledgements and related correspondence, if any.
  • A list of missing records and the people responsible for supplying them.

Use the compliance handover checklist to organise record ownership. Agree a secure transfer method after the initial enquiry; do not send portal passwords or one-time codes through the form.

Related work such as income-tax return filing or bookkeeping and reconciliation needs its own agreed scope where required.

Common questions

Before a response is filed.

Can I send only a screenshot of the notice?

The complete PDF and portal context are normally required. A screenshot may omit the section, annexure, due date, DIN or response instructions.

Does receiving a notice mean a penalty is final?

Not necessarily. The meaning depends on the communication and proceeding. We review the actual notice rather than infer the outcome from a subject line.

Can you guarantee the notice will be closed?

No. Decisions remain with the tax authority and depend on law, facts and evidence. We can define and perform the professional response work within an agreed scope.

Are hearings and appeals included?

Only when expressly included. Further notices, hearings, rectification, appeal drafting, representation and external counsel may require separate scope and fees.

What should I avoid sending?

Do not share portal passwords, OTPs or unrelated sensitive data. We will confirm a record checklist after reviewing the notice.
